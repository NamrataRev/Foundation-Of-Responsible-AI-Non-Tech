# Tokens, Context Windows, Temperature and Top-p

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this topic, you should be able to:

- Explain what a token is and why AI pricing and limits are measured in tokens rather than words
- Describe what a context window is and what happens when a conversation exceeds it
- Choose the right temperature setting for a given AI task — factual, creative, or somewhere in between
- Explain what top-p does and how it relates to temperature
- Apply all three controls together when designing a real AI-powered feature

---

## Overview - Why These Three Controls Matter

Imagine you are building a customer support chatbot for an e-commerce platform. A customer opens the chat and types:

*"Hi, I ordered a pair of shoes last Tuesday, paid $89, but received the wrong size. I want to return them and get a refund. Also, can you tell me how long the refund takes? And do I need to send them back before you process it?"*

You ask your AI model to draft a helpful reply.

Now here is what most beginners do not realise: **before the AI writes a single word, three invisible decisions are already shaping what it can and cannot do.**

- Can the AI even read the full customer message — or is it too long?
- Will the AI remember the entire conversation if the customer asks five more follow-up questions?
- Will the AI write a safe, consistent reply — or something creative and unpredictable?

These three questions are answered by **tokens, context windows, and temperature/top-p**. They are not optional settings you can ignore. They are the controls that determine whether your AI system actually works in production — or quietly fails in ways that are hard to debug.

---

## Tokens — What the AI Actually Reads and Writes

Before the AI can process the customer's message, the text gets broken into small chunks called **tokens**. A token is not a word and not a letter — it is something in between. Short common words are usually one token. Longer words get split. Punctuation marks are often their own token.

**In our customer support example:**

The customer's message — *"Hi, I ordered a pair of shoes last Tuesday, paid $89, but received the wrong size. I want to return them and get a refund. Also, can you tell me how long the refund takes? And do I need to send them back before you process it?"* — is approximately **55 tokens**.

That feels small. But in a real support session, you are not just sending the customer's message. You are also sending:
- A system prompt telling the AI its role and rules
- The company's return policy document
- The customer's order history
- The full conversation so far

Add all of that together and a single API call can easily reach 2,000–4,000 tokens.

**Why tokens matter practically:**

- AI pricing is measured in tokens — not words, not messages. A longer system prompt costs more on every single API call
- If your customer's message plus your system prompt plus the policy document exceeds the model's limit — the AI simply cannot see all of it
- If you are building a chatbot, every token in the conversation history costs money and uses space

> **Quick rule of thumb:** 1 token ≈ 0.75 words in English. So 1,000 words ≈ 1,300 tokens. A detailed company policy document of 3,000 words uses roughly 4,000 tokens — every time you send it to the model.

## Context Window — How Much the AI Can Remember

The context window is the total number of tokens the AI can hold in its memory at once — your system prompt, the entire conversation history, the customer's messages, and the AI's own replies, all counted together.

**Think of it like a whiteboard in a meeting room.** You and the customer are having a conversation, and everything said gets written on the whiteboard. The whiteboard has a fixed size. Once it fills up, you have to erase the oldest content to make room for the new messages. The AI literally cannot see what has been erased.

**In our customer support example:**

The customer starts the chat about the wrong shoe size. You send:
- System prompt: 500 tokens
- Return policy document: 1,200 tokens
- Customer's first message: 55 tokens
- AI's reply: 120 tokens

That is already 1,875 tokens. The customer then asks four more questions. By question six or seven, the earliest parts of the conversation may start disappearing from the model's view — depending on the context window size of the model you are using.

**What this looks like to the customer:**
They say *"as I mentioned earlier, I paid $89 for these shoes"* — and the AI replies as if it has no idea what they paid, asking them to repeat the information. The AI did not forget — it literally cannot see that part of the conversation any more.

**Why context window matters for your design:**

| Model | Approximate Context Window |
|---|---|
| GPT-3.5 | ~16,000 tokens |
| GPT-4o | ~128,000 tokens |
| Claude Sonnet | ~200,000 tokens |

Choosing the right model for your use case includes choosing one whose context window fits your actual conversation length — plus system prompt, plus any documents you inject.

## Temperature — How Predictable or Creative the AI Is

Temperature is a number — usually between 0 and 1, sometimes up to 2 — that controls how the AI chooses its next word from all the options it has calculated.

**Think of it like the confidence dial on a quiz show contestant.**

- Set it low (closer to 0) — the contestant always answers with their safest, most confident guess. Reliable, consistent, rarely surprising.
- Set it high (closer to 1 or above) — the contestant sometimes takes a risk on a less obvious answer. More interesting, more varied — but occasionally wrong.

**In our customer support example:**

**Low temperature (0.1–0.3):**
The AI always writes the clearest, most policy-accurate reply. Every customer who asks the same refund question gets essentially the same response. Predictable, professional, consistent.

> *"Thank you for reaching out. For the wrong item received, you can initiate a return within 30 days. Your refund of $89 will be processed within 5–7 business days after we receive the returned item."*

**High temperature (0.8–1.0):**
The AI generates more varied, conversational replies — different phrasing, different structure each time. Sometimes warmer and more engaging. But occasionally it phrases a policy incorrectly or adds information that was not in the policy document.

> *"Oh no, that's really frustrating — wrong sizes are the worst! Let's get this sorted for you right away. You'll need to pop the shoes back to us, and once we've got them we'll get that $89 back to you within about a week."*

**Which to use:**
For a customer support chatbot — low temperature. Consistency and accuracy matter more than creativity. Reserve higher temperature for tasks like generating multiple marketing taglines or brainstorming ideas, where variety is the goal.

| Temperature | Behaviour | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 – 0.3 | Very consistent, near-deterministic | Factual replies, policy answers, data extraction |
| 0.4 – 0.7 | Balanced — some variation in phrasing | General chat, summaries, explanations |
| 0.8 – 1.5 | Creative, varied, sometimes surprising | Brainstorming, marketing copy, creative writing |

## Top-p — A Smarter Way to Control Variety

Top-p (also called nucleus sampling) is a close cousin of temperature. Instead of controlling how bold the AI is in choosing words, it controls **how many words the AI is even allowed to consider** at each step.

**Using the same quiz show analogy:**
Temperature is like how confident the contestant feels. Top-p is like saying "you can only choose from the answers on this shortlist — ignore everything else."

- **Top-p = 1.0** — the AI considers all possible words. Nothing is excluded.
- **Top-p = 0.9** — the AI only considers the words that together account for 90% of the probability. The very unlikely words are cut off entirely.
- **Top-p = 0.5** — the AI only considers the words that account for 50% of the probability. The output stays very close to the most likely options.

**In our customer support example:**

With **top-p = 0.9:** The AI might vary its phrasing slightly — *"your refund will be processed"* vs *"we will process your refund"* — while always keeping the content accurate and on-policy.

With **top-p = 0.5:** The AI almost always uses the most common, expected phrasing. Very consistent — but can feel slightly robotic over many messages.

**Temperature vs top-p — which to use:**

| Setting | What it controls | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Temperature** | How bold the AI is in its word choices | Adjusting creativity vs consistency overall |
| **Top-p** | Which words the AI can even consider | Fine-tuning variety while keeping output grounded |

In practice, many teams use **temperature for coarse control** and **top-p for fine-tuning**. A common production setting for a customer support bot might be temperature = 0.2 and top-p = 0.9 — predictable enough to stay on-policy, with just enough flexibility to not sound robotic.

> **One important rule:** Do not set both temperature and top-p to their extremes at the same time. Temperature = 0 already makes the output near-deterministic — adding top-p = 0.1 on top adds nothing useful and can cause unexpected behaviour in some models.

## Putting It All Together — Back to the Customer Support Bot

Here is how all three controls interact in one real decision:

**The customer asks:**
*"I ordered shoes last Tuesday, got the wrong size, paid $89. I want a refund — how long will it take and do I need to return them first?"*

**Token impact:**
The message is ~55 tokens. The system prompt is 500 tokens. The return policy is 1,200 tokens. Total context used so far: ~1,755 tokens. Plenty of room for several more exchanges — but every message adds to the cost and the count.

**Context window impact:**
If this customer has a long back-and-forth — say, ten messages each way — the earliest context may start disappearing. A well-designed system keeps the system prompt and the most recent five exchanges visible, and gracefully drops older messages rather than losing the policy document.

**Temperature and top-p impact:**
Temperature = 0.2, top-p = 0.9. The AI replies:

> *"Thank you for contacting us. Since you received the wrong size, you are eligible for a full refund of $89. Please return the shoes using our prepaid label — once we receive them, your refund will appear within 5–7 business days."*

Accurate. Professional. Consistent. The settings did exactly what they were designed to do.

## Best Practices

- Always estimate your token count before building — system prompt + documents + conversation history can add up faster than expected
- Choose your model's context window based on your real use case, not the largest available — larger context windows cost more per call
- Set temperature low for any task where consistency and accuracy matter. Only increase it when variety is genuinely the goal
- Use top-p alongside temperature for fine-grained control — temperature for overall creativity, top-p to keep the output grounded
- Never set both temperature and top-p to extreme values simultaneously

## Common Beginner Mistakes

- **Treating tokens as equivalent to words** — 1,000 words is roughly 1,300 tokens. Documents and system prompts consume far more tokens than people expect
- **Ignoring context window limits** — building a chatbot that sends the full conversation history every time without realising it will eventually exceed the model's context window
- **Setting high temperature for factual tasks** — a customer support bot with temperature = 0.9 will give different (and sometimes wrong) answers to the same question
- **Setting both temperature and top-p to extremes** — one control is usually sufficient. Using both at extreme values can produce unpredictable results

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Tokens** are the unit of measurement for everything an LLM reads and writes — not words, not sentences. Know your token count because it affects cost, limits, and what the model can actually see
- **Context window** is the model's total working memory. Once it fills up, earlier content disappears. Design your system to fit within the window — or to manage which content gets dropped gracefully
- **Temperature** controls how predictable vs creative the AI's output is. Low for consistency. Higher for variety
- **Top-p** controls which words the model even considers at each step. Use it alongside temperature to fine-tune output variety without fully randomising the output
- All three controls interact — a well-designed AI system sets them deliberately based on the task, not by leaving them at defaults

> **Interview tip:** If asked "how would you configure an LLM for a customer support chatbot?" — walk through all three controls: estimate token usage, choose a context window that fits the conversation plus documents, set low temperature for consistency. This shows you think about AI systems as production engineering problems, not just prompt writing.
